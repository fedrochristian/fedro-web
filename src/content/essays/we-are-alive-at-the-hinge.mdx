For three hundred thousand years, a human life was roughly the same shape as the
one before it. The same dawns. The same hunger. The same gods, more or less,
and the same approximate explanations for the weather. A peasant in 800 AD and
a peasant in 800 BC would have understood each other's days in a way neither
would understand ours.

That is over. We are alive at the tipping point.

<PullQuote>
  Intelligence is becoming abundant. Energy is becoming abundant. The two forces
  that bottlenecked every human ambition are coming loose at the same time.
</PullQuote>

You can feel this without knowing the numbers. The rate at which capability
arrives now is not the rate at which institutions move. The rate at which
capability arrives is not the rate at which old jobs retire and new ones become
legible. It is not the rate at which laws are written, marriages adapt, or
faiths absorb new questions. The friction you feel is the friction of a line
that used to be flat curving upward.

## What it does not say

It does not say abundance is inevitable. It does not say collapse is inevitable.
It says only that the steady-state assumption — that next year looks like last
year, plus or minus a percent — has lost its grip. **Variance widens. Tails get
fat.** That is the only reliable statement about the next twenty years that
costs nothing to make.

<Callout title="What the tipping point isn't">
  The tipping point is not "AI." AI is one face of it. So is energy. So is biotech.
  So is the cheapening of compute. The tipping point is the moment several exponentials
  cross the threshold of mattering, at once, and stop being curiosities.
</Callout>

## Why this matters for your week

The temptation, when you read essays like this, is to treat the curve as
cosmic. Something happening to civilizations, somewhere over the horizon, on
nobody's particular Tuesday. It is not. The curve is the reason your job
description is unstable. It is the reason the rules your parents used to plan
their thirties no longer fit the room you are standing in. It is at your door
already.

The publication you are reading exists to take that seriously. To say plainly
what is happening, where the curve actually bends, and what is at stake in
your week, your work, your purpose, your children, and your sense of who you
are.

Read it slowly. We will not panic. We will not sell you a single future.
We will hand you the choice.
